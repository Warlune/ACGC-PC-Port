The minimap renders the actual native background display lists from directly overhead into an OpenGL atlas. It does not use the illustrated pause-map tile lookup. It covers all seven columns in normal builds and all 31 columns in the development world, including the complete west/center/east towns, mountain borders, railway row and full enclosed ocean and saved island. Internal modular ridges use the same mesh as the main view. The yellow marker uses absolute world X/Z across this full extent, including both coastal connections; subtle dividers identify the town boundaries.

Each acre has a 64Ã—64-pixel atlas tile. After a field, terrain, date or texture-mode change, at most two acres bake per drawn frame. Once complete, ordinary frames only composite the cached atlas and live marker. The first fill appears progressively; there is no periodic full-world redraw. Terrain uses fixed overhead lighting, no distance fog and stationary water UVs. Actual foreground actor meshes (trees, houses, residents, dropped items) are not included in this terrain pass. Their floor/background terrain is included; a complete overhead actor renderer remains separate work.

`pc_minimap.c` builds the small orthographic display-list pass at the end of the font stream. Scoped PC tags in the display-list interpreter switch to the atlas and restore the scene framebuffer, viewport and scissor. `pc_minimap_gl.c` composites the cached atlas and live player marker at final output resolution, after scene scaling. Atlas drawing uses an independent depth buffer and never moves the gameplay camera, changes acre streaming or modifies the save. Framebuffer/viewport/scissor, texture bindings and renderer caches are restored. GL resources are released before context shutdown. Disabled, transparent, paused, indoor, title, transition and submenu states hide the overlay.

`minimap_layout_smoke` checks all regions, border/marker geography, passages, three distinct sizes, screen bounds and physical aspect at five output dimensions. `minimap_terrain_smoke` uses a hidden real OpenGL context to test atlas orientation, target isolation, viewport/scissor restoration, terrain opacity, player marker and hidden-overlay behavior. Run both targets in normal and expanded configurations. Full-game appearance is checked separately against an isolated development save.


Readability update: the cached terrain receives a 5Ã—5 weighted smoothing pass once after a bake. Water/land classification keeps the filter from mixing across shorelines. This softens repeated grass/dirt texture patterns without filtering the main game view or adding a per-frame blur. The northern 0.75 acre of off-map river-source scenery is cropped from the minimap; the railway and playable terrain remain in view. Marker geography and aspect calculations use the cropped extent.

Building and landmark symbols come from live foreground anchors across all three towns, including generated villager-house placeholders. Homes, shops, post offices, stations, museums, police, tailors, wells, docks, dumps, tents and lighthouses have distinct vector glyphs. Nearby duplicate dock anchors are combined. Landmark badges take priority in crowded areas; displaced badges have leaders back to their true positions. Badge sizes are bounded in physical output pixels, and villager homes are smaller. Terrain retains the user's opacity; symbols remain at least 94% opaque and the player marker remains bright. Zero opacity and Off hide the entire minimap. Symbols update from foreground data twice per second, but their separate overlay texture only redraws when data or display size changes.

Each capture uses a tag range large enough for all 496 tiles. `tools/aco/render-planning-map.py ATLAS SAVE LAYOUT OUTPUT.png` labels the three towns, ocean and saved island at a uniform acre scale. The legacy 21x10 layout retains its older inset rendering mode. Mainland dock markers also come from port terrain so actor loading cannot temporarily hide the east dock.
