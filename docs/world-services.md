# Three-Town World Services

The OCE3 field contains three native seven-acre towns in a 31-by-18-acre world. The center town keeps the native Nook shop and post office so the opening mortgage and mail progression stay reachable. The west Nook storefront routes to the Night Market, the east storefront routes to the Maritime Outfitter, and one surviving noncenter tailor pad routes to the Customization Shop. The native shop foreground actors and compatible exterior pads remain in place for the PC service layer. See [Night Market](night-market.md) for its stock and hours.

The generator retains exactly one museum, police station, and wishing well across the three towns. It chooses among valid native pads from a stable map-derived seed, so the retained region varies with the map but does not change during a service-only migration. A center tailor is retained when present, with one extra tailor pad reassigned to customization; duplicate landmark and tailor foreground actors are removed without clearing other objects or deposit bits. Each town retains its house-acre combo and all four native player-house plot anchors, and each has at least one dump/scrap-yard pad. Extra post-office rail pads become dump pads while retaining their height bits.

The twelve house plots are physical world locations: four plot anchors per town. The native save still supports four residents and has not been expanded. Twelve concurrent resident records or online players require a separate save and multiplayer migration; these plots alone do not provide that backend.

The center town is always available. The east coast unlocks after a resident pays their first house loan; the west coast unlocks after the second. Unlock progress is shared across existing residents. The gate changes collision at the two town crossings while preserving water and river collision. The center-shop query is also prioritized for native shop progression, so the west storefront cannot capture the starter town's native upgrade or intro-guide lookup.

Manual boating is planned after the third fully paid house loan. Regional fences are therefore pedestrian barriers across the dry walking passages, not walls across the ocean; this milestone does not enable manual boating in the current build.

For an existing valid OCE3 save, service migration preserves the four saved two-acre exploration islands and two wall landings at their exact combination cells and variants. The canonical ferry island and east port remain in their native locations. Fresh marker-free layouts generate new destinations; the saved-destination analyzer only reports stored coordinates and does not reroll them.

The shared rules live in [world-generation.py](../tools/aco/world-generation.py), with the migration entry point in [connect-world-coast.py](../tools/aco/connect-world-coast.py). The final isolated migration is `Y:\ACO\docs\service-final.gci`; its service, island, and verification manifest is `Y:\ACO\docs\services-world-generation-final.json`. The source save was left unchanged.

Validation: `tools/aco/test-world-generation.py` passed 60 native generator seeds. Checks cover service uniqueness and distribution, all twelve house plots, one dump per town, idempotence, valid island spacing and sailing routes, and preservation of an existing OCE3 island/landing layout. The final migration manifest records sixteen passing checks, including unchanged ocean rows 7–14 and save checksum validation.

For native regression testing, run the expanded executable against a disposable save with `--dev-world --dev-shop-test --verbose`. This uses real service anchors, SDL menu events, resident pockets and wallet, roof ownership, and the native design overlay. It restores its in-memory save and clock before exiting. `--dev-shop night|outfitter|studio` starts outside its native entrance; adding `--dev-shop-menu` opens its menu for visual inspection. `--dev-progress-stage 0|1|2` and `--dev-unlock-regions` are nonpersistent development overrides, never mortgage rewards. Keep these switches out of ordinary play launchers.

The native integration run passed night purchase, daytime rejection, insufficient funds, full pockets, outfitter purchases, roof paint, saved-pattern printing, and native design-menu launch. All three storefront menus were visually inspected in the running game; unsupported ASCII punctuation was replaced with native font symbols or plain words. Both normal and expanded Windows builds and the economy, mortgage, and mail-resource smoke executables pass. See [gate validation](world-gates-validation.md) for actual locked and unlocked movement tests and their limits. The source assets and original save are not bundled in Git.

The repurposed services use the original building meshes, recolored per building instance, with replacement artwork confined to the existing nameplates. The original center-town shops retain their original textures. Native doors lead into the original shop or tailor interior and return to the correct regional storefront. The night market uses a distinct Nookling profile with a purple uniform, presented as Milo's Night Market. The outfitter sells working native tools; manual boating remains a separate feature. The studio offers paid roof recoloring (applied on the next town load), umbrellas printed from saved designs, and the native design workbench.

Sign artwork was generated with the imagegen skill from the original texture atlases, then cropped to the nameplate regions and converted to native CI4 indices. Local source images and previews are in `Y:\ACO\texture-work\world-services`. Only the generated sign indices and recolor palettes are compiled into the program; original house textures are loaded from the user's disc. The tailor sign uses a vertical atlas flip before its native UV rotation to make the text read correctly. Region blockers use native long and short picket-fence panels at their original scale, with the original woodgrain texture recolored white. The short panels are rigidly angled into a shallow zigzag so their sprite faces stay visible to the fixed camera. They meet the mountain foot and end at the beach; matching collision covers the small northern gap as well as the main walking passage. Outer cliff corners receive the original ocean model underneath to close the exposed water notch.

The local minimap follows continuous player coordinates, including acre boundaries, while clamping at world edges. Generation also replaces stray inland flat acres in the shoreline row with the native beach combination and collision. Objects and buried-item flags below the replacement shoreline move to vacant northern ground in the same acre. The east-town column-21 repair passed a native `--dev-world --dev-beach-test --verbose` run: approach from z=4220, stop at z=4262, and retreat to z=4188.8. The 60-seed suite checks coastline normalization, object/deposit preservation, and idempotence; minimap layout tests check continuity across both axes.

The native gate regression now waits until the player actually reaches the seam and stops, then reverses input. The October 17 run stopped 18 units before each closed seam and retreated more than 40 units in both directions. A fresh copy of the current shoreline-normalized save stopped at z=4262 and retreated to z=4160.6. Development resident initialization now runs once at boot; returning from an interior preserves the native doorway rather than rerunning the development spawn and daily renewal.

The native interior test also passed after the conversion (`Y:\ACO\native-shop-interior-test\shop-final.log`): each service's room and return location, night purchase, daytime rejection, outfitter tools, paid roof paint, printed pattern umbrellas and the native design workbench. The test pins only its simulated RTC value; production prices and hours still use the ordinary game clock. The cloned Nook rooms hide unrelated native daily-stock pictures but continue to draw player-dropped items. Visual checks of both outer rounded cliff caps confirmed animated water under the previously black notches. The normal and expanded Windows builds, shop smoke test and progression smoke test passed.

The final white fence uses rigid copies of the native short picket sprite with its original UVs and scale. Baking the transforms into copied vertices avoids altering the field matrix, and the small zigzag keeps the sprites visible instead of edge-on. The barrier covers world z=3760–4200, including the former 80-unit gap below the mountain. Six native movement runs passed: both locked crossings at z=3780, 4020 and 4180, each stopping at the seam and allowing retreat. The white pickets were then visually checked from the normal game camera.
